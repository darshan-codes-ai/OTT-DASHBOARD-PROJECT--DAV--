Key Insights & Analytical Synthesis

Five data-backed empirical takeaways synthesizing content volume, platform exclusivity, audience demographic targeting, and quality concentration across the streaming landscape.

01
Exclusivity Distribution

The Dataset Is Characterized by High Catalog Exclusivity

Cross-platform overlap analysis demonstrates that multi-platform co-licensing is exceedingly rare in this dataset. Out of 9,515 unique movie titles:

  • 9,262 titles (97.34%) are flagged exclusively on a single service.
  • Only 245 titles (2.57%) are shared between two platforms.
  • Just 7 titles (0.07%) are shared across three services.
  • Exactly 1 title (Zapped, 2014) is simultaneously available on all four streaming platforms.

Descriptive Finding: With 97.34% of movie titles exclusive to a single platform in this dataset, catalog overlap across services is minimal (only 2.66% appear on two or more services).

02
Volume vs. Quality

Catalog Breadth Does Not Equal High-Rated Concentration

While Prime Video has the largest number of platform-flagged records in this dataset (4,113 movies, or 43.23% of the catalog), Netflix has the largest count of titles in the highest rating tier:

  • Out of 335 total movies rated $\ge 8.0/10$, Netflix hosts 168 titles (50.15%).
  • Prime Video hosts 53 high-rated titles, representing 1.29% of its platform catalog.
  • Disney+, with 922 total titles in this dataset, contains 75 movies rated 8.0+ (an 8.13% proportion).

Descriptive Finding: In this dataset, Prime Video exhibits the largest catalog breadth alongside a lower proportion of high-rated titles (1.29%), whereas Netflix has 168 titles and Disney+ has 75 titles in the RT ≥ 8.0 category. These differences reflect descriptive catalog distributions and should not be interpreted as causal conclusions.

03
Temporal Distribution

Release Year Correlations Differ Across Platform Catalogs

Pearson correlation modeling in Lab 3 demonstrates that release years exhibit distinct directional associations across platforms:

  • Netflix ↔ Year ($r = +0.3066$): A positive correlation showing a higher proportion of recent release years among Netflix titles in this dataset.
  • Prime Video ↔ Year ($r = -0.2437$): A negative correlation reflecting a higher frequency of older twentieth-century releases among Prime Video titles.

Descriptive Finding: Netflix availability correlates positively with recent release years ($r = +0.3066$), whereas Prime Video contains a larger representation of earlier films ($r = -0.2437$). These represent descriptive catalog characteristics rather than causal or strategic claims.

04
Audience Demographics

Adult Content Dominates Rated Catalogs, but Metadata Missingness Is Severe

Analysis of classified content indicates clear audience targeting:

  • Among the 5,338 rated titles, mature audiences (18+) represent the largest single category with 2,276 movies (42.64%).
  • Disney+ diverges almost completely from competitors, focusing almost exclusively on all (698 titles across dataset) and 7+ (1,090 titles across dataset).
  • However, 4,177 titles (43.90%) completely lack standard MPAA age ratings in the dataset.

Academic Caveat: Because unrated titles represent nearly half the dataset, conclusions regarding overall platform maturity must be framed conditionally.

05
Critic Acclaim

Critic Scores Follow Normal Bell Curves with Rare Consensus at the Extremes

Statistical kernel density estimation across the 9,508 non-null Rotten Tomatoes scores reveals that critical evaluations conform closely to Gaussian normality:

  • The distribution mean is 53.55% (5.35 / 10) with a standard deviation of 13.20.
  • The 4–6 rating bin captures 5,429 movies (57.10% of the entire library).
  • Very few titles achieve unanimous critical consensus: only 119 movies (1.25%) score below 20%, and only 335 movies (3.52%) score at or above 80%.

Academic Implication: The bulk of commercial film production is judged as moderately competent by film critics, with genuine masterpieces and unwatchable failures representing statistical outliers.

Academic Scope & Methodological Limitations

Transparent scholarly research requires explicit acknowledgment of dataset constraints and analytical boundaries:

Dataset Limitations & Non-Causal Disclaimers
  • Cross-Sectional Snapshot: The dataset captures a historical snapshot (titles up to 2021) and does not reflect ongoing streaming contract expirations or new original releases.
  • Descriptive, Not Causal: Correlation values (such as between Netflix and Release Year) indicate descriptive catalog associations in this specific sample, not causal mechanisms or corporate curation policies.
  • Absence of Audience Viewing Data: The dataset does not contain viewing figures, streaming hours, subscriber counts, or user engagement metrics. A title's critical acclaim is not equivalent to audience popularity.
  • Excluded Proposal Variables: Preliminary course proposals mentioned attributes such as Genre, IMDb Ratings, Directors, and Runtime; however, these fields are absent from the verified dataset and are deliberately excluded from this research to preserve academic fidelity.
